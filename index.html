HIGHWAY COMMISSION DECLARES SEVEN MILES OF ROUTE 9 "OPTIONAL"

Drivers may elect whether the roadbed exists under their tires or if they prefer to coast upon municipal confidence alone.

Staff Photographer: Brother Vance of the St. Jude Pulley NEGATIVE IDENTIFIER: #904-B

The Board of Public Carriage and Asphalt Alignment met under kerosene lamps this morning to resolve the lingering discrepancy between milepost 41 and milepost 48. Citing "exhaustion in the gravel" and a 20 percent decline in the will of the macadam to sustain locomotion, the Chief Commissioner announced that drivers shall henceforth navigate the stretch entirely as an exercise in personal discretion.

"For twenty-two unbroken years, the Commonwealth has forced gravel to lie horizontal against its fundamental temperament," read the majority memorandum penned by Alderman Crane. "It is unreasonable to insist that the Earth continue offering hardness under the tires of milk trucks simply because men have bought stamps."

Motorists approaching the section at Mile 40 are confronted with two identical painted boards: the left reads "THE HARD PAVEMENT REMAINS FOR PEDANTS", while the right simply offers "THE COLD VOID OF COMPROMISE (SPEED LIMIT 35)". Reports indicate several bakery vans have already vanished into the latter without expressing noticeable grievance.

Local clergy have backed the decree, noting that the parish cemetery has absorbed three inches of additional silence since the asphalt relaxed. Motorists who run out of velocity while traversing the optional sector are advised to remain seated and sing hymns pertaining to standard units of measurement until towed by cattle.
